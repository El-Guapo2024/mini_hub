# Celsius to Fahrenheit Conversions

The exact conversion formulas are:

$$
F = \frac{9}{5}C + 32 \qquad\qquad C = \frac{5}{9}(F-32)
$$

**Shortcut for Celsius to Fahrenheit** (avoids the fraction $\frac{9}{5}\text{)}$:
1. Double the Celsius temperature.
2. Take that doubled value, move its decimal point one place left, and subtract that from the doubled value.
3. Add 32.

**Example:** Convert $20^\circ C$ to Fahrenheit.

$$
20 \times 2 = 40 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 40 - 4 = 36 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 36 + 32 = 68^\circ F
$$

This works because $\frac{9}{5}C = 2C - \frac{2C}{10} = 2C - 0.2C = 1.8C$, so doubling and then subtracting a tenth of the doubled value gives exactly $1.8C$.

**Worth memorizing outright** (no calculation needed):

$$
32^\circ F = 0^\circ C, \qquad 212^\circ F = 100^\circ C, \qquad -40^\circ F = -40^\circ C
$$

That last one is the unique temperature where both scales agree — a common trap answer and a common quiz question by itself.

Going the other direction (Fahrenheit to Celsius), just use $C = \frac{5}{9}(F-32)$ directly: for $98.6^\circ F$, $(98.6-32) = 66.6$, and $\frac{5}{9}\times 66.6 = 37^\circ C$ — body temperature, another value worth remembering by heart.

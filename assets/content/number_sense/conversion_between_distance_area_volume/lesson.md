# Distance-to-Area-to-Volume Conversions

Students find linear conversions relatively simple (for example $1\,\text{ft.} = 12\,\text{in.}\text{)}$, however when asked to find how many cubic inches are in cubic feet, they want to revert back to the linear conversion, which is incorrect ($1\,\text{ft.}^3 \neq 12\,\text{in.}^3\text{)}$. When converting between linear distance to areas and volumes you must square or cube the conversion factor, respectively. So in our example, we know that:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\text{ft.} \\
&= 12\,\text{in.} \implies 1\,\text{ft.}^3 \\
&= (12)^3\,\text{in.}^3 \\
&= \mathbf{1728}\,\text{in.}^3
\end{aligned}
$$

Another example converting $\text{ft.}^2$ to $\text{yd.}^2$ is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\text{yd.} \\
&= 3\,\text{ft.} \implies 1\,\text{yd.}^2 \\
&= (3)^2\,\text{ft.}^2 \\
&= \mathbf{9}\,\text{ft.}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

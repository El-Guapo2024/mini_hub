# Converting Decimals

In the same manner as integers in base-10, decimals in base-10 can be expressed as sums of negative powers of 10. For example:

$$
\begin{aligned}
.125 \\
&= 1 \cdot (.1) + 2 \cdot (.01) + 5 \cdot (.001) \\
&= 1 \cdot 10^{-1} + 2 \cdot 10^{-2} + 5 \cdot 10^{-3}
\end{aligned}
$$

This can be displayed in terms of fractions as well:

$$
\begin{aligned}
 \\
&= \frac{1}{10} + \frac{2}{100} + \frac{5}{1000} \\
&= \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{50} + \frac{1}{200} \\
&= \frac{20 + 4 + 1}{200} \\
&= \frac{1}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

Similar to integer base conversion, we can replace the powers of ten with powers of any base fraction. For example, converting $.321_6$ to a base-10 fraction:

$$
.321_6 = \frac{3}{6} + \frac{2}{36} + \frac{1}{216} = \frac{108 + 12 + 1}{216} = \frac{121}{216}
$$

Because of the complexity of the calculations involved, going in the reverse direction (base-10 fraction to base-$n$ decimal) is seldom (if ever) used on a number sense test. The test usually asks for a base-10 fraction representation — be sure to reduce!

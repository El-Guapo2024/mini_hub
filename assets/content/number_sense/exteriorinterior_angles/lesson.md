# Exterior/Interior Angles

When finding the exterior, interior, or the sum of exterior or interior angles of a regular $n\text{-gon}$, you can use the following formulas:

- **Sum of Exterior Angles:** $360^\circ$
- **Exterior Angle:** $\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$
- **Interior Angle:** $180^\circ - \dfrac{360^\circ}{n} = \dfrac{180^\circ(n-2)}{n}$
- **Sum of Interior Angles:** $n \cdot \dfrac{180^\circ(n-2)}{n} = 180(n-2)$

If you were to only remember one of the above formulas, let it be that the sum of the exterior angles of every regular polygon is equal to 360. From there you can derive the rest relatively swiftly (although it is *highly* recommended that you have all formulas memorized).

**Example:** Find the sum of the interior angles of an octagon.

**Solution:** $\sum = 180(8-2) = \mathbf{1080}$.

In order to find the interior angle from the exterior angle, you used the fact that they are supplements. Both supplements and complements of angles appear on the number sense test every now and then, so here are their definitions:

- Complement of $\theta = 90^\circ - \theta$
- Supplement of $\theta = 180^\circ - \theta$

# Finding a Remainder when Dividing by 11

Finding the remainder when dividing by 11 is very similar to finding the remainder when dividing by 9 with one catch: you add up alternating digits (beginning with the ones digits) then subtract the sum of the remaining digits. Let's look at an example to illustrate the trick:

$13542 \div 11$ has what remainder?

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{Sum of the Alternating Digits:} & (2 + 5 + 1) = 8 \\
\text{Sum of the Remaining Digits:} & (4 + 3) = 7 \\
\text{Remainder:} & 8 - 7 = \mathbf{1}
\end{array}
$$

Sometimes adding then subtracting "down the digits" will be easier than finding two explicit sums then subtracting. For example, if we were finding the remainder of $3456789 \div 11$, instead of doing $(9 + 7 + 5 + 3) - (8 + 6 + 4) = 24 - 18 = \mathbf{6}$ it might be easier to do $9 - 8 + 7 - 6 + 5 - 4 + 3 = 1 + 1 + 1 + 3 = \mathbf{6}$. That is what is so great about number sense tricks, there are always methods and approaches to making them faster!

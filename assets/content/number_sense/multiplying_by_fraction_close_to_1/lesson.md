# Multiplying by a Fraction Close to 1

This trick — essentially clever factoring — applies whenever a whole number is multiplied by a fraction close to 1. Rewrite the fraction as $1 \pm \text{(small number)}$ and distribute:

$$
\begin{aligned}
14 \times \frac{15}{16} \\
&= 14 \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{16}\right) \\
&= 14 - \frac{14}{16} \\
&= 14\frac{7}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

The same procedure works when the fraction is slightly above 1:

$$
\begin{aligned}
17 \times \frac{13}{11} \\
&= 17 \times \left(1 + \frac{2}{11}\right) \\
&= 17 + \frac{34}{11} \\
&= 20\frac{1}{11}
\end{aligned}
$$

Note: when the fractional part is improper (as in the second example), you must reduce it to a mixed number to get the correct answer.

Also, when the whole number equals the numerator of the fraction, you can apply the $a \times \frac{a}{b}$ trick from Section 1.3.9 for a potentially quicker path — but this method always works regardless.

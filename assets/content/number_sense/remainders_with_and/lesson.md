# Remainders with a/p, b/p, and ab/p

It's best just to show a practice problem first so you know the type of question being discussed:

**Problem:** If $\dfrac{6x}{7}$ has a remainder of 2 and $\dfrac{2y}{7}$ has a remainder of 3, what is the remainder of $\dfrac{4xy}{7}$?

As explained in the Remainders of Expressions topic, "the remainders after algebra is equal to the algebra of the remainders." You can do the multiplication of the first two expressions which translates to the multiplication of the remainders. From there, you can divide the calculated expression (and their equivalent calculated remainders) to get what the question is asking for:

$$
\frac{6x}{7} \times \frac{2y}{7} = \frac{12xy}{7} \div 3 = \frac{4xy}{7}
$$

Same operations with equivalent remainders:

$$
2 \times 3 = 6 \div 3 = \mathbf{2}
$$

Now sometimes when you do the algebra on the remainders, you wind up with a fractional answer. You can either figure out small, individual values for $x$ and $y$ and use them in the problem expression to calculate the remainder, or you can think about what $xy$ fits the expression. Here is an example to show both methods:

**Problem:** If $\dfrac{2x}{7}$ has a remainder of 1 and $\dfrac{y}{7}$ has a remainder of 3, what is the remainder of $\dfrac{xy}{7}$?

*Solution 1:* Multiplying the first two expressions and dividing by 2 yields: $\dfrac{xy}{7} \cong 1.5$, which is a roadblock. From the first expression, run through small values of $x$ to find $x = 4\text{;}$ do the same for the second expression to find $y = 3\text{;}$ therefore $\dfrac{xy}{7} = \dfrac{12}{7}$ which has a remainder of **5**.

*Solution 2:* Again, multiplying the first two expressions and dividing by 2 yields: $\dfrac{xy}{7} \cong 1.5$. Multiplying by 2 gives $\dfrac{2xy}{7} \cong 3$. Treating $xy$ together and running through small values yields $xy = 5$, therefore $\dfrac{xy}{7} = \dfrac{5}{7}$ which has a remainder of **5**.

Both methods work, so it's up to you to find which way you are more comfortable with.

# Roman Numerals

The following are the roman numerals commonly tested on the exam:

$$
\begin{array}{llll}
\text{I} = 1 & \text{V} = 5 & \text{X} = 10 & \text{L} = 50 \\
\text{C} = 100 & \text{D} = 500 & \text{M} = 1000 &
\end{array}
$$

Knowing the above table and also the fact that you arrange the numerals in order from greatest to least ($M \to I\text{)}$ with the exception of one rule: you can't put four of the same numerals consecutively. For example, to express 42 in roman numerals it would not be 42 = XXXXII, it would be 42 = XLII. To circumvent the problem of putting four of the same numerals consecutively, you use a method of "subtraction." Anytime a numeral of lesser value is placed in front of a numeral of greater value, you subtract from the larger numeral the small numeral. So in our case 40 is represented by XL $= 50 - 10 = 40$. When converting numbers, it is best to think of the number as a sum of ones, tens, hundreds, etc. units. For example, to express 199 in roman numerals: The way you want to look at it is $199 = 100 + 90 + 9$ then express each one as a roman numeral. So $100 = \text{C}$, $90 = \text{XC}$, and $9 = \text{IX}$, so $199 = \textbf{CXCIX}$.

**Arithmetic on numerals.** Most of the questions here are not conversions but sums, differences, products and quotients written in numerals — $\text{CXI} - \text{CC}$, $\text{XXVII} \times \text{CXI}$, $\text{DCII} \div \text{IX}$. There is no trick to doing the arithmetic in Roman: convert each numeral to a number, work in ordinary digits, and give the answer as a number. The answer is never wanted back in numerals, so do not spend time converting it.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{CM} + \text{XC} + \text{IX} &= 900 + 90 + 9 = \mathbf{999} \\
\text{DC} - \text{LX} - \text{VI} &= 600 - 60 - 6 = \mathbf{534} \\
\text{CXI} - \text{CC} &= 111 - 200 = \mathbf{-89}
\end{aligned}
$$

The subtraction rule is where these go wrong: read $\text{CM}$ as $1000-100=900$, not as $100+1000$. Check each numeral for a smaller symbol sitting in front of a larger one before you add anything up.

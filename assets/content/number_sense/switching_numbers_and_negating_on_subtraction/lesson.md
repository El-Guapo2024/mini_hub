# Switching Numbers and Negating on Subtraction

Far too common, students make a mistake when subtracting two fractions whose result is a negative answer. An example of this is $4\frac{5}{6} - 10\frac{11}{12}$. Most of the time, it is incredibly easier switching the order of the subtraction then negating the answer. Taking the above problem as an example:

$$
\begin{array}{rl}
4\dfrac{5}{6} - 10\dfrac{11}{12} &= -\!\left(10\dfrac{11}{12} - 4\dfrac{5}{6}\right) \\[8pt]
&= -\!\left(10\dfrac{11}{12} - 4\dfrac{10}{12}\right) \\[8pt]
&= -\!\left(6\dfrac{\mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{12}}\right)
\end{array}
$$

Here is another example to illustrate the same point:

$$
\begin{array}{rl}
2\dfrac{5}{6} - 4\dfrac{2}{3} &= -\!\left(4\dfrac{2}{3} - 2\dfrac{5}{6}\right) \\[8pt]
&= -\!\left(4\dfrac{4}{6} - 2\dfrac{5}{6}\right) \\[8pt]
&= -\!\left(1\dfrac{\mathbf{5}}{\mathbf{6}}\right)
\end{array}
$$

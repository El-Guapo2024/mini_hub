# Combinations Formula

For most, this is just a refresher on the definitions of Combinations ($_{n}C_{k}\text{)}$ and Permutations ($_{n}P_{k}\text{)}$:

$$
_{n}C_{k} = \frac{n!}{k! \cdot (n-k)!}
$$

$$
_{n}P_{k} = \frac{n!}{(n-k)!}
$$

**Example:**

$$
_{7}C_{4} = \frac{7!}{4!(7-4)!} = \frac{7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5}{3 \cdot 2} = \mathbf{35}
$$

With combinations and permutations (and factorials in general) you want to look at ways of canceling factors from the factorial to ease in calculation. In addition, the following is a list of the factorials which should be memorized for quick access:

$$
\begin{array}{llll}
3! = 6 & 4! = 24 & 5! = 120 & 6! = 720 \\
7! = 5040 & 8! = 40320 & 9! = 362880 & 10! = 3628800
\end{array}
$$

Another often tested principle on Combinations is that:

$$
_{n}C_{k} = {_{n}C_{n-k}}
$$

The above will show up in the form of questions like this: $_{5}C_{2} = {_{5}C_{k}} \rightarrow k = ?$

**Solution:** Using the above formula, you know that $k = 5 - 2 = \mathbf{3}$.

Another often tested question on Combinations and Permutations is when you divide one by another:

$$
\frac{_{n}C_{k}}{_{n}P_{k}} = \frac{1}{k!} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{_{n}P_{k}}{_{n}C_{k}} = k!
$$

# Special Trig Angles Table

Trigonometry problems have been increasingly popular for writers of the number sense test. Not only are they testing the basics of sines, cosines, and tangents of special angles ($30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ$ — and variations in each quadrant) but also the trigonometric reciprocals (cosecant, secant, and cotangent).

First, the special angles in the first quadrant where all values are positive:

$$
\begin{array}{llllll}
\textbf{Trig Function} & \mathbf{0^\circ} & \mathbf{30^\circ} & \mathbf{45^\circ} & \mathbf{60^\circ} & \mathbf{90^\circ} \\[4pt]
\sin & 0 & \dfrac{1}{2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & 1 \\[10pt]
\csc & \text{Undefined} & 2 & \sqrt{2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{3} \cdot 2}{3} & 1 \\[10pt]
\cos & 1 & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \dfrac{1}{2} & 0 \\[10pt]
\sec & 1 & \dfrac{\sqrt{3} \cdot 2}{3} & \sqrt{2} & 2 & \text{Undefined} \\[10pt]
\tan & 0 & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} & 1 & \sqrt{3} & \text{Undefined} \\[10pt]
\cot & \text{Undefined} & \sqrt{3} & 1 & \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} & 0
\end{array}
$$

All of those can be derived using the memorable "SOHCAHTOA" technique applied to special right triangles. In addition, it is clear that the values at the reciprocal trigonometric function is just the multiplicative inverse (that's why they are called *reciprocal* trigonometric functions!).

To find the values of trigonometric functions in any quadrant it is essential to remember two things. The first is the sign of the values depending on what quadrant you are in. The mnemonic "**A**ll **S**tudents **T**ake **C**alculus" tells you where each function is positive:

- Quadrant I: **All** functions positive
- Quadrant II: **Sin** (and csc) positive
- Quadrant III: **Tan** (and cot) positive
- Quadrant IV: **Cos** (and sec) positive

The second challenge is learning how to reference each angle to its first quadrant angle. The reference angle for $\theta$ in each quadrant is:

$$
\begin{array}{lllll}
 & \textbf{Quadrant I} & \textbf{Quadrant II} & \textbf{Quadrant III} & \textbf{Quadrant IV} \\[4pt]
\text{Reference Angle:} & \theta & 180^\circ - \theta & \theta - 180^\circ & 360^\circ - \theta
\end{array}
$$

**Example:** $\sin(210^\circ)$

*Solution:* The angle is in Quadrant III, so the result will be negative (only cosine is positive in Q-III). The reference angle is $\theta - 180^\circ = 210^\circ - 180^\circ = 30^\circ$. So $\sin(30^\circ) = \frac{1}{2}$, thus $\sin(210^\circ) = -\frac{1}{2}$.

**Example:** $\cot(135^\circ)$

*Solution:* The cot/tan function is negative in Q-II. The reference angle is $180^\circ - \theta = 180^\circ - 135^\circ = 45^\circ$. Now $\cot(45^\circ) = 1$, so $\cot(135^\circ) = \mathbf{-1}$.

**Example:** $\cos(-30^\circ)$

*Solution:* An angle of $-30^\circ = 330^\circ$ which is in Q-IV where cosine is positive. The reference angle is $360^\circ - \theta = 360^\circ - 330^\circ = 30^\circ$, and $\cos(30^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. So $\cos(-30^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

Students should familiarize themselves with using radians as well using the conversion rate of $\pi = 180^\circ$. So an angle (given in radians) of $\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{180^\circ}{6} = 30^\circ$.

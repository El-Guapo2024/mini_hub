# Fundamental Trig Identities

Recently, questions involving trigonometric functions have encompassed some basic trigonometric identities. The most popular ones tested are included here:

**The Fundamental Identities**

$$
\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1
$$

$$
1 + \cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta
$$

$$
\tan^2\theta + 1 = \sec^2\theta
$$

**Sum to Difference Formulas**

$$
\sin(a \pm b) = \sin(a)\cos(b) \pm \sin(b)\cos(a)
$$

$$
\cos(a \pm b) = \cos(a)\cos(b) \mp \sin(a)\sin(b)
$$

**Double Angle Formulas**

$$
\sin(2a) = 2\sin(a)\cos(a)
$$

$$
\cos(2a) = \cos^2(a) - \sin^2(a) \quad \text{with variants:}
$$

$$
\cos(2a) = 1 - 2\sin^2(a)
$$

$$
\cos(2a) = 2\cos^2(a) - 1
$$

**Sine $\to$ Cosine**

$$
\sin(90^\circ - \theta) = \cos(\theta)
$$

Most of the time, using trigonometric identities will not only aid in speed but will also be necessary. For example:

$$
\sin(10^\circ)\cos(20^\circ) + \sin(20^\circ)\cos(10^\circ)
$$

Without using the sum to difference formula, this would be impossible to calculate, however after using the formula you get:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin(10^\circ)\cos(20^\circ) + \sin(20^\circ)\cos(10^\circ) \\
&= \sin(10^\circ + 20^\circ) \\
&= \sin(30^\circ) \\
&= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

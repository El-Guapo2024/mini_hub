# Factorial Fractions: (a! ± b!)/c!

Despite the name, problems of this type have little to do with factorials and are mostly about basic fraction simplification.

The key strategy is to split or factor the expression. For example, when the denominator divides cleanly into one of the numerator factorials:

$$
\frac{8! + 6!}{7!} = \frac{8!}{7!} + \frac{6!}{7!} = 8 + \frac{1}{7} = 8\frac{1}{7}
$$

Sometimes it is easier to factor out the smallest factorial from the numerator:

$$
\frac{3! + 4! - 5!}{3!} = \frac{3! \cdot (1 + 4 - 5 \cdot 4)}{3!} = 1 + 4 - 20 = \mathbf{-15}
$$

The general approach is:
1. If the denominator is between the factorials in the numerator, split the fraction into separate terms and simplify each.
2. If the denominator is the smallest factorial present, factor it out of the numerator and cancel.
